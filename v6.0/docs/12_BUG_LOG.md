No obvious wiring error was found through visual inspection.### Possible CausesThe exact cause was not confirmed. Possible causes included:* no motor voltage reaching the TB6612FNG VM input;* incorrect emergency-stop NC-terminal wiring;* poor contact in the fuse or fuse holder;* insufficient current from the motor power supply;* an unreliable common-ground connection;* incorrect STBY, PWM or direction-control signals;* poor contact between the driver output and motor wires;* a damaged or incorrectly connected motor-driver module.### Status**Unresolved — original TB6612FNG design paused**---## Bug 2: Insufficient Diagnostic Equipment### ProblemI could inspect the wiring visually, but I could not determine where the electrical signal or power path had failed.### CauseI did not have a multimeter or another suitable electrical testing device.### EffectI could not verify:* supply voltage;* fuse continuity;* emergency-stop continuity;* common ground;* motor-driver input voltage;* motor-driver output voltage.Without these measurements, replacing individual components would only be based on guesses.### SolutionI decided to obtain a digital multimeter and include measurement checkpoints in the revised Stage 4 procedure.### Status**Diagnostic limitation identified; equipment added to the revised plan**---## Bug 3: Motor Speed Was Initially Suspected Incorrectly### ProblemI suspected that the speed value of 40 might have been too low to start the motor.### TestI increased the speed value to 70 and repeated the command.### ResultThe motor still did not move.### ConclusionThe failure was probably not caused only by a low speed value.
